If you run a plumbing company in Katy, your website has one job: convince a homeowner standing in a wet hallway that you're close, you're real, and you'll pick up. That means a tap-to-call number at the top, service area pages that cover the ZIPs people actually use, and recent proof from jobs in neighborhoods they recognize. Everything else on the site is secondary.

Katy is not Houston proper and it doesn't behave like it. Worth understanding why before you spend a dollar on a site.

## Katy is bigger than the City of Katy — and your site needs to know that

Here's the quirk that trips up most plumber websites out here. A huge number of homes with a "Katy, TX" mailing address aren't inside Katy city limits at all. They're in unincorporated Harris County or Fort Bend County, spread across the 77449, 77450, 77493 and 77494 ZIPs. The address says Katy. The county line says something else.

That matters because your customer doesn't care about jurisdiction. She tells her neighbor she lives in Katy, she types "plumber near me in Katy," and she expects the results to be people who work her street.

So your site should speak the way she does. One service area page that lists the ZIPs you cover, the master-planned communities you work in regularly, and the landmarks people use to orient themselves — the Grand Parkway, I-10, Old Katy. Not a generic "We serve the Greater Houston area" line that could mean anything.

## You're competing against Houston-sized ad budgets

Type "plumber Katy TX" and you won't just see Katy plumbers. You'll see big Houston operations and national franchise brands that run ads into the whole west side. They have call centers. They have budgets you're not going to out-spend.

What they don't have is a truck that's already fifteen minutes away.

Your advantage is being genuinely local, and your website is where you prove it. Not with a stock photo of a smiling guy in a clean uniform — with the stuff only a local would put on the page. Which communities you're in most weeks. What your response time actually looks like on the west side versus crossing town. Whether you're licensed and insured and who's answering at nine at night.

A Katy homeowner who's been burned by a dispatcher in another state reads that and relaxes. That's the whole game.

## Most Katy jobs start as a recommendation, not a search

This is the part a lot of plumbers underestimate. Katy's big master-planned communities — Cinco Ranch and the rest — run on neighborhood groups. Somebody posts "water heater died, who do you use," and gets eleven replies in an hour.

Then what happens? Every one of those people Googles the names.

So the search that wins you the job often isn't "plumber near me." It's your business name typed into a phone by someone who already half-trusts you because a neighbor vouched. If what comes back is a Facebook page with three posts from 2021, you've lost the referral your work earned.

What that person needs to see in about eight seconds:

- Your name and phone number, big, tappable
- That you're actually in Katy or serving it directly
- Reviews, recent ones, with dates
- Photos of real work — your truck, your crew, a finished job
- Hours, and whether you do emergencies

That's it. They're not reading your About page. They're confirming you exist and dialing.

## Build pages around what Katy homeowners actually call about

The housing stock out here splits in two directions, and your service pages should follow it.

Newer construction in the master-planned areas generates its own set of calls — fixture issues, pressure problems, water heater replacements as the first round of builder-grade units ages out, gas line work for outdoor kitchens and pool heaters. Older homes closer to Old Katy and the original parts of town bring sewer line, cast iron, and repipe conversations.

And across all of it: slab leaks. The clay soil through this part of Texas moves with the weather, and movement finds pipe. "Slab leak detection Katy" is a real search by a real person with a real water bill problem, and it deserves its own page — not a bullet point buried on a services list.

Same with drainage and water heater replacement. One page each, written plainly: what the symptoms are, what you do about it, what happens next. A page that answers the question is a page Google will show.

## Phone-first, because nobody's at a desk

Almost every plumbing search in Katy happens on a phone, and half of them happen from somewhere inconvenient — the garage, the driveway, the parking lot at work in the Energy Corridor on the drive home.

So design for a thumb:

- Phone number in the header, tappable, on every page
- Nothing important hidden behind a menu
- No pop-up asking for an email before they can read anything
- Short forms. Name, phone, what's wrong, address. Four fields.
- Fast load. If the page takes five seconds on cell service, they're already dialing the next guy.

## Get Google Business Profile right before you touch anything else

The map pack sits above the regular results, and for plumbing it's where most of the clicks go. Your website supports it — it doesn't replace it.

Claim the profile. Set the service area honestly. Pick the right primary category. Add photos regularly, not once. And ask for reviews consistently — after the job, while the customer is relieved and grateful, by text with a direct link.

One Katy-specific note: if you're a service-area business without a storefront, hide your address rather than listing your house. It's the correct setup and it avoids problems later.

## What drives the cost up or down

Nobody can quote a plumbing job blind, and nobody should quote a website blind either. But here's what moves the number.

Cost goes **up** with page count — a site with eight service pages and four area pages is more work than a five-page site. Custom photography costs more than using what's on your phone, though honestly your phone photos of real jobs often convert better than studio shots. Booking systems, dispatch integrations, and anything that has to talk to your field software add complexity.

Cost goes **down** when you come prepared: your logo, your license numbers, a list of services, and a folder of job photos. Half the delay in most website projects is waiting on the owner to send material.

Watch for the ongoing stuff — hosting, updates, who fixes it when something breaks at 11pm. Ask what's included and what's billed separately before you sign. More on evaluating that in [Website Designer Near Sugar Land: What to Look For](/blog/website-designer-near-sugar-land/).

Keep reviews fresh, because the referral always gets Googled. And load fast, because your customer is standing in water.
