## The short answer

To get your Katy business on Google, claim and verify a free Google Business Profile, pick the right primary category, set your service area to the parts of Katy you actually cover, and start collecting reviews from real customers. Then point it at a website that loads fast on a phone and names the areas you serve. That combination is what puts you in the map results when someone in Cinco Ranch searches "AC repair near me."

That's the whole recipe. The rest of this is the part nobody tells you — why Katy is harder than it looks, and what to do about it.

## Katy is bigger than the city of Katy, and Google knows it

Here's the thing that trips up almost every owner out here. A "Katy, TX" mailing address stretches across three counties — Harris, Fort Bend, and Waller — and covers a huge chunk of ground west of the Grand Parkway. Most people who say they live in Katy don't live inside the city limits at all.

Why that matters: Google's map results lean heavily on how close you are to the person searching. Someone searching from a subdivision out past the Grand Parkway and someone searching from Old Katy near the railroad are standing miles apart. They can get completely different results for the same words.

So if your shop sits on one side of that spread, you may be invisible on the other side — even if you've been driving out there for jobs for ten years. You don't fix that by wishing. You fix it with service area settings, review volume, and a website that actually mentions the places you work.

## Claim the profile first. Everything else hangs off it.

Go to Google Business Profile and search your business name. One of three things happens:

**It already exists and you don't control it.** Common for businesses that have been around a while — Google auto-generates listings from phone directories and old data. Click "Claim this business" and verify.

**It exists and a former employee or old marketing company owns it.** You can request access. They get an email and a window to respond. If they ignore it, you can take it over. Annoying, but doable.

**Nothing's there.** Create it from scratch.

Verification is usually a video these days — Google asks you to film your truck, your signage, your tools, your business location. Have your wrapped truck, your equipment, and something with your address on it ready before you start. If you're a home-based contractor in one of the Katy-area subdivisions, that's fine; just hide your address and set a service area instead.

## Category and service area: the two settings that decide everything

Your **primary category** is the single biggest lever on the profile. "Air conditioning contractor" and "HVAC contractor" pull different results. "Fence contractor" and "fence supply store" are not the same thing. Pick the one that matches the job you most want to be called for, not the broadest one. Add secondary categories for the rest.

Your **service area** should be honest. List the parts of greater Katy you'll actually drive to, plus whatever else you cover — Fulshear, Brookshire, the Energy Corridor stretch along I-10, Richmond, wherever. Don't list all of Harris County because you're hoping for more reach. Google doesn't reward that, and you'll waste time on calls two counties away.

Then fill out everything else. Hours. Phone number that rings a human. Services list with real descriptions. Photos — actual job photos from Katy properties, not stock images of someone else's kitchen. A new fence on a Katy-area lot, a foundation pier job, a pool resurface, a plate of what you actually serve. Google shows photo-heavy profiles more often, and customers click them more.

## Reviews are the scoreboard in a market this competitive

Katy's hard because you're not only competing with other Katy businesses. You're competing with Houston-based companies that list Katy as a service area and spend real money on ads. A lot of them are good. Some of them subcontract the work and you'd do a better job.

The one thing you can beat them on is local review volume. A Houston company with 400 reviews spread across the whole metro doesn't necessarily outrank a Katy company with 60 reviews from customers in the neighborhoods right around you.

Ask every single customer. Not "leave us a review sometime" — send the direct link by text before you pull out of the driveway. Google gives you a short review link right in your profile dashboard. Save it as a text shortcut on your phone.

And reply to all of them. Even the one-stars, especially the one-stars. A calm, specific reply to a bad review does more for you than the review does against you.

## The website behind the profile still decides whether you get the call

A Google Business Profile gets you seen. Your website is where someone decides if you're worth calling. And the people doing that deciding in Katy are usually on a phone — standing in a garage looking at a dead water heater, or sitting in traffic on I-10 on the way home from the Energy Corridor at 6 p.m.

That means:

- Phone number at the top, tap to call, no hunting
- Loads in a couple of seconds on cell service, not Wi-Fi
- Says what you do and where, in the first screen
- Pages for the areas you serve, written like a human, not a list of zip codes
- Photos of your own work

That last one about service pages matters more in Katy than in a tighter market, because of the geographic spread. One page that says "Serving Katy and surrounding areas" doesn't give Google much. Real pages about the work you do in specific communities do.

## Keep it consistent everywhere else

Your name, address, and phone number should match character-for-character across your profile, your website, Facebook, Yelp, Nextdoor, the Better Business Bureau, and any trade directories you're in. "Suite B" on one and "Ste. B" on another is minor. A disconnected old phone number floating around on three directories is not — it splits your signal and sends customers to a dead line.

Spend an afternoon searching your own business name and cleaning up what you find.

## A realistic first month

- **Week 1:** Claim and verify the profile. Set category and service area. Add hours and phone.
- **Week 2:** Load 20+ real job photos. Write out your services. Add your website link.
- **Week 3:** Start texting the review link to every finished job. Reply to everything that comes in.
- **Week 4:** Clean up old listings. Look at your site on your own phone and fix whatever makes you wait.

You won't rank overnight. New profiles take a while to earn trust, and Katy has a steady stream of new residents moving into new construction who are picking contractors for the first time — that's a continuous flow of searches, and you want to be positioned before the next wave. Three to six months of doing this consistently moves most businesses.
